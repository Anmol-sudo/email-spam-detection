SLIDE 5 — WHAT THE DATA REVEALED (EDA)
Headline: Spam looks different before you even read it.
Two-column comparison table, SPAM in red vs HAM in green:
• Length: spam is roughly 2× longer on average
• Uppercase: far more capital letters (WIN, FREE, URGENT)
• Digits: shortcodes and prize amounts — 87121, £1000
• Links: URLs are heavily concentrated in spam
• Typical words — spam: free, call, txt, claim, prize, mobile, win
• Typical words — ham: ok, got, come, home, later, love, time
Caption: Two word clouds that barely overlap. The classes really are separable
by vocabulary alone — which is exactly why a bag-of-words model works here.
Visual: two facing word clouds, red and green.

SLIDE 6 — TURNING TEXT INTO NUMBERS
Headline: A model cannot read. It can only count.
Show as a left-to-right pipeline diagram with 7 small steps:
lowercase → strip URLs → strip digits → strip punctuation → tokenize →
remove stopwords → lemmatize
Then a second row showing the vectorization:
cleaned text → TF-IDF (20,000 terms, unigrams + bigrams) → sparse vector
Before/after example in a code-style box:
  Before: "FREE entry in 2 a wkly comp to win FA Cup final tkts 21st May 2005"
  After:  "free entry wkly comp win fa cup final tkts may"
Two callouts:
• TF-IDF = Term Frequency × Inverse Document Frequency — rare, distinctive words
  score high; words that appear everywhere get damped.
• Bigrams let the model learn "free entry" and "call now" as single phrases,
  not just isolated words.

---

SLIDE 7 — CHOOSING A MODEL WITH EVIDENCE
Headline: Six algorithms, five metrics, one winner.
Table with a checkmark or medal on the winning row:
Logistic Regression · Naive Bayes · Linear SVM · Random Forest ·
Gradient Boosting · KNN
Winner callout: Logistic Regression
Three reasons, as icon cards:
• Accuracy on par with the best (Linear SVM tied on F1)
• Returns true probabilities — an SVM cannot, and the app's confidence score
  and sensitivity slider both depend on them
• Every coefficient is directly readable, which is what makes the explanations
  in slide 10 possible
Footnote: KNN was included as a deliberate control — distance is nearly
meaningless in 20,000-dimensional sparse space, and its score showed it.

---

SLIDE 8 — TUNING & CLASS IMBALANCE
Two-column layout.
LEFT — Hyperparameter tuning:
• GridSearchCV over 36 configurations × 5 folds = 180 model fits
• Searched TF-IDF settings AND model settings together, inside one Pipeline
• Scored on F1, not accuracy — accuracy would have tuned toward "say ham more"
• Winning setup: 20,000 features, bigrams, C = 10.0, L2
RIGHT — Class imbalance:
• SMOTE generates synthetic spam examples — it interpolates between real ones
  rather than duplicating them
• Applied to the TRAINING set only; the test set must stay 13% spam, like reality
• Compared: no balancing vs class_weight='balanced' vs SMOTE
• Honest result: balancing raised recall but cost precision, so the final
  shipped model does not use SMOTE. A measured decision, not an assumption.
Bottom callout: Using a Pipeline was what prevented data leakage — the
vectorizer is refitted inside every cross-validation fold.

SLIDE 10 — EXPLAINABLE AI
Headline: The model shows its work.
Explain that logistic regression scores a message as
intercept + Σ (TF-IDF value × coefficient), so each word's exact contribution
can be computed — no approximation needed.
Two columns of learned weights, red and green:
• Strongest spam indicators: txt (−9.12), claim (−7.71), mobile (−7.68),
  service (−6.75), reply (−6.61), prize (−5.80), win (−5.01), free (−4.77),
  urgent (−4.50), cash (−4.47)
• Strongest ham indicators: ok (+3.92), home (+2.94), road (+2.86), say (+2.69),
  later (+2.60), good (+2.52), way (+2.42)
Callout: The model independently rediscovered every word a human would list for
SMS spam — and nobody told it any of them.
Note: LIME (Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations) was also
implemented in the notebook for model-agnostic explanations; the app uses the
exact coefficient method because the model is linear, which is both more
faithful and far faster.
